Probably the most stereotypical gripe about living in the city:

The slow walkers.

And genuinely, it’s not even about being slow.

It’s the lack of spatial awareness.

Like, hey, we’re a group of six. Let’s all walk next to each other and take up the entire sidewalk so absolutely no one can pass us.

Or the swerving.

If you want to walk slowly, that’s totally fine. Just pick a freaking lane so I can get around you.

And don’t even get me started on the escalators.

Right is to stand. Left is to walk.

How in the world do you see literally everyone standing on the right side and then decide that *you* should stand on the left?

I fear I am definitely in a few tourists’ “New York horror stories” about getting yelled at by a mean New Yorker.

But babes, when I hear my train pulling into the station and the next one isn’t for fifteen minutes, you bet I am going to tell you to move to the right.

Now, I don’t want you to think I’m always full-speed walking all the time.

I love a leisurely stroll.

But it’s about the time and place.

You may have the freedom and time to casually wander down the sidewalk, but the person next to you might very well be late to an important meeting, an audition, a date, or God knows what else.

So just make it easy for them to pass you.

My siblings say it’s such a blessing that I don’t drive because I don’t get attacked for my road rage.

Unfortunately, I have walking rage.

And I have, in fact, almost gotten into a fight because of it once or twice.

Oops.

My closest call?

I was taking the train uptown with my friend. We get to our stop and go to exit, and this little guy cuts me off, putting himself directly between me and my friend.

As someone who tends to zip through crowds, I don’t mind at all.

I simply keep walking.

Everyone funnels toward the stairs, which are one of those single-file exits, and we get to the first step.

And this man…

FULL STOPS.

I almost knock him down.

Not on purpose. Just because, again, we’re in a single-file line and everyone is moving.

So I say:

“Move.”

He continues standing there like a deer in headlights.

I repeat:

“Move.”

Nothing.

So I maybe, *maybe,* a little too sternly ask:

“What in the world are you doing? GO.”

Finally, I get past him, catch up to my friend, and start leaving.

And then this man decides to run past me while cussing me out the entire time.

At this point, I start very loudly explaining to my friend what happened and how unbelievably dumb what he just did was.

Honestly, had she not been there trying to chill the situation, I probably would’ve gotten hit.

Oops.

And the funny thing is, I really wasn’t even upset until he decided to stop.

Because why would you cut someone off and then FULL STOP?

I understand if you don’t know where you’re going.

That’s fine.

But if you commit, you need to commit.

Keep moving until you can get out of the way.

Then stop.

Then figure out where you’re going.

This is New York.

We are all going somewhere.

Anyways, maybe I’m the jerk.

That’s your call.

But if you see a girl speed-walking down the sidewalk with a look of absolute fury on her face, babes…

Just move.

-love, the girl in 3C
