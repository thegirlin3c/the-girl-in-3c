People love to talk about the “old” New York.

How it was grittier. How people talked to each other. How there was more community. How it felt alive in a way that it supposedly doesn’t anymore.

And while maybe New York will never be exactly the same as it once was, I think it’s a hell of a lot more alive than people give it credit for.

I accidentally did a little experiment.

I lost my headphones.

For an entire week.

And the week I went without constantly blocking out the city was, genuinely, one of the best weeks I’ve had in a long time.

Not because everything was suddenly wonderful.

Actually, quite the opposite.

I heard everything.

The good, the bad, the weird, the annoying, and the completely insane.

But that’s kind of what made it so good.

One day, I was wearing a Giants shirt and a very sweet Brooklyn native guy started talking to me on the train.

And when I say talking, I mean *yapping.*

We talked about the Giants for basically our entire train ride.

It was great.

Another day, I helped someone carry their stroller down the stairs.

Nothing dramatic. No big conversation. I helped, they said thank you, and we both went on with our days.

And then there was the other side of New York.

The crazy woman screaming slurs and insults at a girl sitting next to me.

The usual zombie shoving his way through the train, clicking and clacking as he pushed past everyone.

The screaming fight that broke out when a girl couldn’t squish herself onto a rush-hour train.

Normally, I would’ve just put my headphones in.

Music on.

World off.

But instead, I was immersed in it.

And something kind of magical happened.

The girl who was being verbally attacked got off at the same stop as me.

I started talking to her.

And she ended up crying and hugging me because, as she put it, she was “fine,” but it was nice to be heard and seen.

When the zombie came barreling through the train, another girl and I made eye contact and shared a little nod.

That silent, *I got your back.* look that every New Yorker knows.

And after the screaming match, when the doors finally closed, an older couple who had squeezed onto the train next to me told me how happy they were that I had made it on.

Because I deserved to make it.

It was such a small thing.

But I remember it.

And that’s what surprised me most about the whole experiment.

The “bad” moments were actually some of the moments that made me feel the most connected to the people around me.

Because suddenly I wasn’t just passing thousands of strangers every day.

I was *with* them.

We were all experiencing the same ridiculous city together.

The woman crying.

The couple trying to squeeze onto the train.

The girl getting screamed at.

The person helping someone with a stroller.

All of us just… living.

All of us figuring our lives out at the same time.

Maybe that’s what people mean when they say they’re missing the old New York.

Maybe they’re not actually missing the old New York.

Maybe they’re missing feeling connected to the people in it.

And maybe we’re making that harder than it needs to be.

Sometimes you just have to take out the headphones.

Look around.

Talk to someone.

Help someone.

Make eye contact with the girl across the train when something absolutely insane happens.

Let yourself hear the city.

Because New York is still talking.

We just aren’t always listening.

Did this experiment convince me to completely give up my headphones?

Truthfully, most of the time.

I genuinely love my little home for everything it has to offer.

The good, the bad, and the completely unhinged.

Although on the days when I’m already feeling easily overwhelmed, overstimulated, or just not in the mood to hear someone screaming on the train at 8:30 in the morning…

I’m putting my headphones in.

I’m only human.

-love, the girl in 3C
